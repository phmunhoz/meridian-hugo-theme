## The context

The client was a fashion e-commerce with a consolidated operation — solid revenue, experienced team, well-defined processes. The problem wasn't the business: it was how much it cost to keep it running.

Between licensing, agency fees for day-to-day maintenance, and custom integrations that had become their own projects, the annual platform cost was consuming a disproportionate slice of margin. And any change to the store required going through the agency — with timelines measured in days or weeks.

## The challenge

This wasn't a simple store. There were years of accumulated customizations, integrations with ERP and logistics systems, a large catalog, and an order history that needed to be preserved — all without stopping sales.

## How it was done

The process started by understanding what existed, not by deciding what to build. Each customization was evaluated: migrate, replace with a native solution, or discard.

Discovery and architecture took the first four weeks. Construction happened in parallel with the existing store fully operational. The cutover was executed in a controlled window with monitoring for the first days after go-live.

## Results

The most immediate change was autonomy. The marketing team started making changes directly — content edits, promotions, shipping configuration — without filing a single ticket.

Organic traffic held steady thanks to the SEO migration plan implemented alongside the technical work. Three months after go-live, the client began configuring advanced automations — something that wasn't viable on the previous platform without custom development.
